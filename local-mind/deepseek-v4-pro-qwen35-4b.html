Measured on M1 Ultra Q4_K_M Provisional: external disk, host under load

104.2 tok/s decode, 1,552–1,619 t/s prefill on an M1 Ultra 128 GB, from a 2.58 GiB Q4_K_M GGUF through stock llama.cpp. Measured 2026-11-18, two independent runs — and provisional: the weights were read over USB on a host under memory pressure, so this entry keeps the catalogue's unevaluated badge rather than clearing it.

A 4B text model from the Qwen3.5 line, distilled by Jackrong and further fine-tuned on roughly 250,000 DeepSeek-V4-Pro reasoning traces. The xtools-at checkpoint catalogued here is the decensored ("heretic") derivative of that, produced with Heretic v2.0.0.

Decode is quoted as one figure because the two runs agree to 0.6%. Prefill is quoted as a range rather than a point, because the two runs differ by 4% and the second carried a ±136 spread against the first's ±0.5 — a single number would hide that the prefill measurement is the noisier of the two.

The decode rate implies about 289 GB/s of weight traffic: 2.58 GiB read once per token, 104 times a second. That is 36% of this machine's ~800 GB/s, which is a normal figure for a small model on a shared-memory GPU — at this size the step is short enough that launch overhead and the per-token fixed costs are a meaningful share, so the remaining 64% is not a defect to chase.

This is a small model chosen for a specific job, not a general assistant. Its published evaluations are maths and science: GSM8K 91.77% (mean of two runs), MMLU-Pro Maths 80.40%, Physics 74.20%, Chemistry 74.80%, for a 76.47% MMLU-Pro average across those three. Those are the publisher's numbers, not measured here.

The decensoring, stated plainly

The checkpoint is a "heretic" variant, and its own card gives the trade: refusals fall from 99/100 to 5/100 on the card's probe set, at a cost of KL divergence 0.0378 from the original. That is not zero, and it is the reason to prefer the parent checkpoint if the decensoring is not what you want. The benchmarks above are the parent's lineage; the decensored file is not separately re-scored on them.

Why a Q4_K_M and not something larger

At 4.33 B parameters the file is 2.58 GiB at Q4_K_M and would be under 9 GiB at Q8. On a 128 GB machine either fits whole in the GPU, so this is not a capacity decision. The Q4_K_M is catalogued because it is the conversion that was verified to load and run here; a larger quantisation would trade decode rate for quality and has not been measured on this machine.

How this was measured, and what that does not cover

Two llama-bench runs with five repetitions each against the same file, on the same machine, in one sitting. The benchmark drives the model directly rather than through a server, so the figure is the engine's own decode loop and carries no HTTP, scheduling or tokenisation overhead. It is a short-context decode rate: the model was warm at 512 tokens, not at 250,000, and a rate at the far end of its window would be lower.

Two conditions do not match the catalogue's own measurement rule, and they are recorded rather than smoothed over. The weights were read from a USB volume rather than internal storage, and the host was under memory pressure during the runs — several GiB of swap in use, with a virtual machine resident. Both are conditions this site's catalogue page says move a result by more than the differences worth reporting. They matter least for decode, because llama-bench reads the file once at load and the 2.58 GiB is then resident, so the transfer path affects load time rather than the per-token loop; and they matter most for prefill, which is the figure whose two runs disagree. The decode rate is therefore the more portable of the two numbers here, and the prefill range should be treated as provisional until it is re-taken on internal storage on a quiet machine.

Not claimed: any quality evaluation run here, any comparison against a different engine on these weights, and any speed for the vision path.
