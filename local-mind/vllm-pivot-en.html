Rust-generated kernels inside vLLM-Ascend

Inside vLLM-Ascend, individual AscendC kernels are swapped for kernels compiled from Rust by tile-rs, one op class at a time, each gated on bit-parity and a measured win.

A

vLLM-Ascend keeps the framework; tile-rs replaces kernels. The seam is proven — our kernels have served a live model path, and the override is additive by construction: anything it does not cover falls through to stock, at 0.89 µs per declined call. Speed is where it stands or falls. On RMSNorm every path measured slower than the operator it replaces, so that override now ships default-off rather than delegating at a cost. The operative override is the MoE gmm1 op, 1.17–1.19× faster than stock at the decode shape it serves — but a census of real serving found that shape does not occur, so today it delegates every call. The win is real and not yet reachable; the measurements below say why.

vLLM-Ascend's kernels are replaceable units

The DS4 path is custom AscendC op packages in csrc/ — DSA sparse attention, lightning indexer, MoE grouped-GEMM+SwiGLU, rms_norm_dynamic_quant — registered as torch.ops._C_ascend.*. Replaceable units, not a monolith.

Our toolchain speaks the same language: tile-rs emits AscendC C++ and already builds .so kernels with the same ascendc_library CMake machinery vllm-ascend uses; launches share the same aclrtStream.

Measured, not estimated — and we are slower. Fused bf16 rms_norm vs npu_add_rms_norm, alternating blocks, correctness re-gated first: M=1 292 vs 68 µs (0.23×), M=16 83 vs 65 (0.78×), M=128 81 vs 41 (0.51×), M=1024 82 vs 46 (0.56×). E2 ✓

Per-kernel A/B — done, and the answer is negative

Both headline kernels are correct and both are slower: fused rms_norm 0.23–0.78× stock, w8a8 GEMM chain 0.33–0.40×. Reported as a range, because the stock arm is what moves — our timings are stable while stock varies ~21%. The flat-cost signature (K2.6) turns this from a dead end into a named target: remove per-call overhead, do not tune the arithmetic.

Gate: ≥1 kernel class at parity or better
E3 ✓

Tile-search retune — landed

Two kernels retuned, both parity-gated first. rms_norm: block-partitioned row loop + barrier elision, 5.53× → 1.25×, now bit-identical to stock. The w8a8 MoE kernel gains 7.08× from blockDim alone (128.8µs → 18.2µs), saturating exactly at N/Nb = 8 blocks as the partitioning predicts. Correctness re-gated at every blockDim — identical error at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, which also proves the partition is disjoint.

Gate met: parity re-checked at every blockDim
E4 ✓

End-to-end A/B — seam proven, and the win is not reachable at today's shapes

A served DeepSeek-V4-Flash request has gone through a tile-rs kernel (fused bf16 add_rms_norm: 5742 hits / 727059 rows on a live engine), and the census of real serving closed the shape question: gmm1 arrives at 32 experts and never 16 rows, so the decode-shaped kernel declines by contract. The decisive follow-up found where decode time actually goes. Under eager execution the pass was 148 ms wall with <1 ms of device compute — the rest sat in collectives whose 30–70 ms wait bands were host-side launch drift between ranks, proven by a 124 µs flat HCCL floor and an algorithm sweep that could not reproduce them. Enabling ACL graph capture removed the drift: the same engine decoded 32 tokens in 1.8 s = 17.8 tok/s single-stream — 4.3× eager and ≈4.9× the originally measured stock baseline — with the vendor fused-dispatch flag adding a further verified +8–11% at both shapes.

Gate: seam proven end-to-end; the served shape is now measured, and this kernel does not cover it

risks & asks

Risks we name up front

R1
CANN version tension: DS4-capable vLLM-Ascend (v0.20.2rc1+) pins CANN 9.0.x while the deployment box is frozen at CANN 8.5.2 — whether the CANN-9 container userland runs on an 8.5-era driver is still untested, and that risk stands. What is no longer speculation is our side of it: a 9.0-built tile-rs kernel ran unmodified on an 8.0 runtime, with only the host wrapper rebuilt.
R2
Quant format gap: Vehicle blocked, not the kernels: no DS4-Flash checkpoint we have tried completes a forward pass — three now, across two boxes and two serving stacks — and in every case the cause sits outside this plugin. w8a8 loads and runs, then fails inside vLLM-Ascend's DSA attention — npu_transpose_batchmatmul rejecting an int8 weight (Tensor x2 not implemented for DT_INT8); a control run with our override disabled fails identically, which is how we know it is not ours. fp8 0731 does not initialise at all: customize_dtype is not supported by the current soc version, raised while casting expert weights, and gated on SoC version inside torch_npu rather than by anything configurable. MXFP4 was added on 2026-08-29 and is the one that is not vLLM's: on a 910B2 A2 node, through llama.cpp's own CANN backend, the 145 GB checkpoint loads across four cards and then aborts in ggml_cann_rope — the runtime cannot load the AI Core kernel for aclnnArange (rtsFuncGetByName ErrCode=107000), although that operator passes test-backend-ops standalone on all four of those cards. Three checkpoints, two stacks, two boxes, three different Ascend-side operator failures and none of them ours. This is what blocks the end-to-end number — the op-level results below stand on their own.
R3
Large-batch GEMM: Beating the stock tuned grouped-GEMM at big prefill shapes is not yet evidenced; the credible opening is decode / small-M, and E2 reports both honestly.
